# Value Investing Framework (Buffett 3M)

## Overview

Value investing evaluates stocks as **partial business ownership**, not trading instruments. Returns come from the business's real earnings power, not from predicting the next buyer's price. The 3M framework filters investments through three sequential gates: **Moat → Management → Margin of Safety**.

## When to Use

Use when the user asks to:
- Analyze a stock or company for investment potential
- Evaluate competitive advantages (moat) of a business
- Determine fair value or intrinsic value of a company
- Assess whether a stock price offers sufficient margin of safety
- Review an investment portfolio or holdings
- Apply Buffett/Munger principles to a specific situation
- Read and interpret financial statements for investment decisions

Do NOT use for: short-term trading, technical analysis, macro forecasting, cryptocurrency speculation, or leverage-based strategies.

## The 3M Framework (Quick Reference)

Apply these three filters in order. Fail any one → exclude or wait.

### Moat (Competitive Advantage)
Does the company have a durable competitive advantage that will persist for 10+ years?

**Five moat types:**
| Type | Signal |
|------|--------|
| Brand | Consumers seek the brand, pay premium, won't substitute |
| Switching Cost | Too expensive/painful for customers to leave |
| Network Effect | Value grows as more users join |
| Cost Advantage | Can produce cheaper than competitors sustainably |
| Regulatory/Patent | Government-granted exclusivity |

**Quick test:** If the company raised prices 5%, would most customers stay? Past 5 years gross margin stable or rising?

### Management
Are the people running the business capable, honest, and shareholder-oriented?

**Four dimensions:** Competence (did they deliver on promises?), Integrity (do they admit mistakes?), Shareholder orientation (do they act like owners?), Capital allocation (how do they use retained profits?).

**Red flags:** CEO sells stock frequently, auditor changes often, related-party transactions, aggressive accounting, acquisitions destroying value.

### Margin of Safety
Is the current price significantly below intrinsic value?

- Estimate intrinsic value as a **range** (not a precise number)
- Target at least 30% discount to the lower bound of that range
- Higher-quality businesses can accept somewhat smaller margins
- Cash is a call option — wait for the right price, sometimes for years

## The Core Mental Model

```
All stocks → Moat filter (~500 pass) → Management filter (~100 pass) → Price filter (≤20 at any time) → Portfolio (5-10 stocks)
```

**Foundational principles:**
- "Buying a stock = buying part of a business" — think like an owner
- "Mr. Market" is your servant, not your guide — exploit extreme prices
- Circle of competence: only invest in what you truly understand
- Preferred holding period: forever (sell only when thesis breaks or extreme overvaluation)
- Never use leverage; always keep cash reserves

## Quick Reference: Key Metrics

| What to Check | Healthy Signal | Warning Signal |
|---------------|---------------|----------------|
| Gross margin trend | Stable or rising 5+ years | Declining 3+ consecutive years |
| ROIC | Consistently > 15% | Below cost of capital |
| FCF / Net Income | > 80% | Persistently negative FCF |
| CapEx / Net Income | < 50% | Rising without revenue growth |
| Debt | Net cash or low leverage | Debt rising faster than earnings |

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ Equating low P/E with "undervalued" (could be a value trap)
- ❌ Buying great companies at any price (Coke at 60x P/E → decade of zero returns)
- ❌ Confusing familiarity with understanding (using a product ≠ understanding the business)
- ❌ Selling because the price dropped (if thesis intact, it's a better buy)
- ❌ Trying to time the market macro-economically (focus on micro: the specific business)
- ❌ Over-diversifying (5-10 well-understood stocks beat 50 superficial picks)
