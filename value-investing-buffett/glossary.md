# Value Investing Glossary

> Unified definitions for key concepts — avoiding confusion from "same term, different understanding."

---

## C

### Circle of Competence (能力圈)
The range of industries and businesses an investor truly understands. **"The size of the circle doesn't matter; knowing its boundary does"** (Buffett). Only invest within your circle — if you can't predict what a company looks like in ten years, the answer isn't "guess," it's "don't invest."

### Compound Interest (复利)
Returns generating additional returns. Formula: FV = PV × (1 + r)^n. The key variables are **r (return rate)** and **n (time)**. The cruelest fact: the vast majority of returns occur in the final years — hence long-term holding is essential. Buffett: "Life is like a snowball. The important thing is finding wet snow and a really long hill."

---

## E

### Economic Goodwill (经济商誉)
The present value of a business's earnings power exceeding its tangible book value. Distinct from accounting goodwill — economic goodwill is the monetized expression of real, sustainable competitive advantages. Accounting goodwill (an accounting number, amortized yearly) ≠ economic goodwill (genuine competitive advantage, which may actually **increase** yearly).

### Economic Moat (护城河)
A company's sustainable competitive advantage protecting its market share and profits from competitors. Five types: brand, switching cost, network effect, cost advantage, regulatory/patent. Buffett coined the castle analogy in his 1993 Shareholder Letter.

---

## F

### Float (浮存金)
Funds held by insurers between receiving premiums and paying claims. Essentially "other people's money temporarily in your care" — available to invest and earn returns. If underwriting is profitable, float has a negative cost — someone **pays you to manage their money**. Buffett first systematically explained this in his 1967 Shareholder Letter. Berkshire's float grew from $17 million (1967) to over $160 billion (2023) — the core fuel of the compounding engine.

### Free Cash Flow / FCF (自由现金流)
Cash from operations minus capital expenditures required to maintain competitive position. FCF is "the money the business can truly distribute to shareholders." Buffett's self-defined "owner earnings" approximates FCF. Formula: Operating Cash Flow − Maintenance CapEx.

---

## G

### Graham, Benjamin (格雷厄姆)
Father of value investing (1894–1976), Buffett's mentor. Author of *Security Analysis* (1934) and *The Intelligent Investor* (1949). Core contributions: intrinsic value concept, Mr. Market allegory, margin of safety principle.

### Gross Margin (毛利率)
(Revenue − Cost of Goods Sold) ÷ Revenue. Measures a company's **pricing power** and product competitiveness. High and stable gross margin → likely has a moat. Sustained decline → moat may be narrowing. One of the most important metrics for value investors.

---

## I

### Intrinsic Value (内在价值)
The discounted value of all cash flows a business will generate over its remaining life. Intrinsic value is independent of market price — it's the value investor's "anchor." The core difficulty: intrinsic value depends on judgments about the future. **"It's an estimate, not a precise number"** (Buffett).

---

## M

### Margin of Safety (安全边际)
The gap between intrinsic value and market price. Buying significantly below intrinsic value provides a cushion for estimation errors. Graham introduced it in *The Intelligent Investor*, Chapter 20. Buffett's analogy: "You build a bridge to hold 30,000 pounds but only drive 10,000-pound trucks across."

### Mr. Market (市场先生)
Graham's metaphor: imagine the stock market as an emotionally unstable business partner who daily quotes buy and sell prices. You can exploit his extreme moods (buy when he's depressed, sell when he's euphoric) but must not be controlled by him. Source: *The Intelligent Investor*, Chapter 8; Buffett elaborated in his 1987 Shareholder Letter.

### Munger, Charlie (芒格)
Buffett's partner of 50+ years (1924–2023), Berkshire Hathaway Vice Chairman. Core contribution: evolving Buffett's philosophy from "buy fair companies at wonderful prices" to "buy wonderful companies at fair prices." Also: multi-disciplinary mental models, inversion thinking, investment checklists.

---

## O

### Owner Earnings (所有者收益)
Buffett's custom metric, approximating true shareholder earnings:

```
Owner Earnings = Net Income + Depreciation & Amortization − Maintenance CapEx
```

### Owner's Mindset (企业所有权思维)
Viewing stock investment as **buying partial ownership of a business**, not trading a piece of paper. "If you aren't willing to own a stock for ten years, don't even think about owning it for ten minutes" (Buffett).

---

## P

### P/B — Price-to-Book Ratio (市净率)
Stock price ÷ book value per share. The core valuation metric of the Graham era (seeking P/B < 1 "cigar butts"). Under Munger's influence, Buffett shifted focus from P/B to ROE and moat — the rise of intangible assets massively reduced P/B's effectiveness.

### P/E — Price-to-Earnings Ratio (市盈率)
Stock price ÷ earnings per share. The most commonly used valuation metric. Low P/E ≠ cheap (could be a value trap — earnings are shrinking); high P/E ≠ expensive (could be a high-growth company yet to reach profitability). Must be judged in conjunction with moat and growth prospects.

---

## R

### Return on Invested Capital / ROIC (资本回报率)
After-tax operating profit ÷ invested capital (interest-bearing debt + equity − excess cash). Measures how well a company uses capital to generate returns **independent of leverage**. Buffett prefers ROIC over ROE — because ROE can be artificially inflated by adding debt.

---

## T

### The $1 Test (一美元前提)
Buffett's management assessment standard: "For every dollar of profit retained by the company, has at least $1 of market value been created?" Long-term, if retained profits' investment returns fall below shareholders' opportunity cost, management should pay dividends instead.

### The 3M Framework (3M)
Buffett's three-dimensional investment filter: **Moat** (sustainable competitive advantage) → **Management** (capable, honest, shareholder-oriented) → **Margin of Safety** (price significantly below intrinsic value). The three filters applied sequentially create a strict investment funnel.

---

## V

### Value Investing (价值投资)
An investment philosophy centered on **business intrinsic value**. Core premise: buying a stock = buying partial ownership of a business; buy below intrinsic value; exploit market fluctuations rather than being exploited by them. Three foundational principles: intrinsic value, margin of safety, circle of competence.
