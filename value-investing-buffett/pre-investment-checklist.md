# Pre-Investment Checklist

> **"You should do all the work before you buy, not after."**
> — Charlie Munger

## How to Use

Complete every item before any significant investment decision. Don't skip any item — the one you skip is always where the problem hides.

Each check has three results:
- ✅ Pass — proceed to next item
- ❌ Fail — exclude or wait
- ⚠️ Unsure — means you haven't researched enough; go back to learning mode

---

## Step 1: Circle of Competence Check

- [ ] **Can I explain how this company makes money in three sentences?** (A 12-year-old should understand)
- [ ] **Do I know who its main customers are, and why they choose it over competitors?**
- [ ] **Do I understand the industry value chain?** Who's upstream? Downstream? How are profits distributed across the chain?
- [ ] **Can I predict roughly what this company will look like in ten years?** If "unsure" → exclude
- [ ] **Am I interested because "everyone else is buying"?** If yes → stop, establish independent judgment

---

## Step 2: Moat Check

### Existence
- [ ] **Does this company have a moat?** (Brand, switching cost, network effect, cost advantage, regulatory — at least one)
- [ ] **Can I clearly articulate the moat's source?** (Can't articulate = likely doesn't exist)
- [ ] **Have gross margins been stable or rising over the past 5 years?** (Declining trend = moat narrowing)

### Durability
- [ ] **Will this moat still exist in ten years?** (Tech moats are most fragile; brand moats are most durable)
- [ ] **Is there an obvious threat that could destroy this moat in 3-5 years?** (Tech disruption? Regulatory change? Consumer preference shift?)
- [ ] **Are competitors eroding its market share?** (Check 3-5 year trends)

### Financial Evidence
- [ ] **ROE consistently > 15% over 5 years?** (If yes, moat has financial proof)
- [ ] **Operating cash flow consistently > net income?** (Earnings quality is high)
- [ ] **CapEx / Net Income < 50%?** (Doesn't need massive ongoing investment to maintain position)

---

## Step 3: Management Check

### Competence
- [ ] **Has management delivered on strategic goals promised 5 years ago?**
- [ ] **Where does the company rank in its industry?** Share growing or shrinking?
- [ ] **Is the CEO/founder deeply involved in operations?** (Cashing out and leaving = huge red flag)

### Integrity
- [ ] **Has management candidly discussed mistakes and challenges in annual reports?** (All sunshine and rainbows = 🔴)
- [ ] **Are accounting policies conservative?** (Compare with industry peers)
- [ ] **Has the auditor been stable?** (Frequent changes = 🔴)
- [ ] **Are there related-party transactions?** (If yes, is there a legitimate business reason?)

### Shareholder Orientation
- [ ] **How much stock does the CEO hold?** (More is better)
- [ ] **Is equity compensation reasonable?** (Dilution rate? Performance hurdles?)
- [ ] **Does retained profit create at least equivalent market value growth?** (The $1 Test)

### Capital Allocation
- [ ] **How does the company deploy its profits?**
  - Reinvest in core business (best)
  - Dividends (good)
  - Buybacks when undervalued (good)
  - Acquisitions (caution — most destroy value)
  - Blind diversification (🔴)
- [ ] **If the company makes acquisitions, are prices reasonable?** How much goodwill has accumulated?

---

## Step 4: Valuation & Margin of Safety

### Estimate Intrinsic Value
- [ ] **Have I estimated a conservative intrinsic value range?** (Not a precise number — a range)
- [ ] **Are my assumptions sufficiently conservative?** (Growth rate below historical average? Cyclicality considered? Management error possible?)

### Calculate Margin of Safety
- [ ] **What discount does the current price offer versus my lower-bound intrinsic value estimate?**
  - 30%+ → can consider
  - 20-30% → research OK, wait for better price
  - Below 20% → wait (unless exceptional quality business)
- [ ] **If my valuation is off by 30%, would I still avoid loss?** If not → margin insufficient

---

## Step 5: Psychological & Behavioral Check

The most important check — your brain is your biggest risk factor.

### Confirm You're Not in "Emotion Mode"
- [ ] **Am I in a FOMO (fear of missing out) state?** Price surging, want to "get in quick" → ⚠️ stop
- [ ] **Am I in a panic state?** Market crashing, want to "just sell first" → ⚠️ reexamine fundamentals
- [ ] **Am I buying because "everyone is buying"?** That's exactly when you may need to wait

### Confirm Independent Judgment
- [ ] **Can I write 3 negative arguments about this company?** (Can't → haven't researched thoroughly enough)
- [ ] **If someone debated me that this is a terrible investment, could I rebut with evidence?** (No → your confidence may be blind)
- [ ] **If the stock dropped 30% right after buying, is my first reaction "add more" or "regret"?** ("Regret" → you may overestimate how well you understand this business)

---

## Step 6: Position Sizing & Portfolio Check

- [ ] **Is the position size reasonable relative to total assets?** (Single stock ≤ what you can tolerate permanent loss on; imagine your portfolio has only 6 stocks)
- [ ] **Am I using leverage?** If yes → 🔴 Stop immediately. Buffett and Munger unanimously oppose leverage.
- [ ] **After this investment, do I still have enough cash for living expenses and future opportunities?** Never go all in — keep dry powder.
- [ ] **How diversified is my portfolio across industries?** If a systemic risk hits one sector, are losses tolerable?

---

## Final Confirmation

Before buying, write down (under 500 words):

1. Why am I buying this company?
2. Under what conditions would I sell?
3. What's my expected holding period?
4. Worst case: how much could I lose? Can I endure that?
