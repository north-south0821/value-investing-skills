# 01 — Value Investing Philosophy

> **"Price is what you pay. Value is what you get."**
> — Warren Buffett, 2008 Shareholder Letter

## Core Idea

Value investing is not a trading strategy — it's a **worldview**. Its fundamental premise:

> **Buying a stock = Buying partial ownership of a business**

When you buy a share, you're not buying an electronic certificate or a point on a chart — you become a **part owner** of that company. Your long-term return depends on the company's **real earning power**, not what the next buyer is willing to pay.

### Stocks vs Businesses: A Thought Experiment

Suppose the stock market closes for ten years tomorrow. Would you still buy this company's shares?

If "yes" → you're analyzing it as a business. If "not sure" → you're speculating.

## Intrinsic Value

Buffett learned from Benjamin Graham:

> **Intrinsic Value** = The discounted value of all cash a business will generate over its remaining life

Key insights:
- Intrinsic value is **independent** of stock price
- Market price fluctuates around intrinsic value, but **converges over the long term**
- The investor's job: estimate intrinsic value, wait until price falls significantly below it

### Plain-English Understanding

Imagine buying an entire restaurant. Your bid should be based on:
- ✓ How much the restaurant earns each year
- ✓ How many years it can operate stably
- ✓ Whether it has repeat customers (a moat)
- ✗ What your neighbor paid yesterday
- ✗ Whether someone will pay more next week

The stock market gives us a privilege: every day, "Mr. Market" quotes prices for our businesses. He's sometimes euphoric (extremely high prices), sometimes depressed (extremely low prices). We can **use** him, but must not be **controlled** by him.

## Why Value Investing Works

### 1. Markets Are Not Always Efficient

The Efficient Market Hypothesis claims prices reflect all information. But Buffett points out: if markets were always efficient, how could the same stock fall from 100 to 50 and back to 100 within a year? Business value cannot change that dramatically that quickly.

Markets price most stocks reasonably most of the time — but precisely at extreme moments (panic or euphoria), pricing deviates severely from value. This is exactly the value investor's window of opportunity.

### 2. The Power of Compounding

Investing 100,000 at 15% annualized return:
- 10 years: ~400,000
- 20 years: ~1.6 million
- 30 years: ~6.6 million
- 50 years: ~108 million

Buffett earned 99% of his wealth after age 50. Not because he got smarter — compounding needs time.

> **"Life is like a snowball. The important thing is finding wet snow and a really long hill."** — Buffett

"Wet snow" = high-ROIC businesses, "Long hill" = enough time.

## How to Practice

### Step 1: Change Your Mental Framework

| Speculator Mindset | Investor Mindset |
|-------------------|------------------|
| Will this stock rise next week? | Will this company exist in 10 years? |
| Where's my stop-loss? | The cheaper it gets, the more I want to buy |
| What's the hot sector? | Which industry can I truly understand? |
| What's the price target? | What's the approximate intrinsic value range? |

### Step 2: Learn to Estimate Intrinsic Value

No need for decimal-point precision. Buffett said:

> **"It's better to be approximately right than precisely wrong."**

Simplified approach:
1. Look at **average free cash flow** over the past 5-10 years
2. Judge whether cash flow is **sustainable** (determined by moat)
3. Estimate future cash flows with **conservative** growth assumptions
4. Discount at your **opportunity cost** (e.g., long-term government bond rate + risk premium)

### Step 3: Patiently Wait for Margin of Safety

Even if you're certain a company is worth 100, don't buy at 95. Wait for market panic, buy in the 50-70 range. This wait could last years — **cash is also a position**.

## Common Misconceptions

- ❌ **Value investing = buying low P/E stocks** — Low P/E could mean a dying business (value trap). Value investing = quality + price, not just cheap.
- ❌ **Great companies can be bought at any price** — Coke at 60x P/E in 2000 delivered near-zero returns for a decade. Great company + bad price = mediocre returns.
- ❌ **Value investing = hold forever, never sell** — Buffett sold IBM (mistake acknowledged), airlines (industry fundamentals changed). The holding period isn't "forever no matter what."
- ❌ **You need complex DCF models** — Buffett says he never uses spreadsheets for valuation. He relies on **qualitative understanding**: will this business exist in 10 years? Will it earn more?
- ❌ **Value investing is obsolete in the tech era** — Amazon, Apple, Tencent all have moats and can be analyzed with value frameworks. Value investing isn't "don't buy tech" — it's "don't buy what you don't understand."
